TIME DILATION SIMULATOR — PHYSICS DETAILS

Gravitational Time Dilation

According to Einstein's general relativity and the Schwarzschild solution, clocks run slower in stronger gravitational fields. A clock near a black hole runs noticeably slower compared to a distant observer.

Schwarzschild Radius

rs = 2GM/c²
  • rs = Schwarzschild radius (event horizon)
  • G = gravitational constant = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²
  • M = mass of black hole
  • c = speed of light = 299,792,458 m/s
  • The MASS slider spans 0.1–5.0 M☉. At 5 M☉, rs ≈ 14.77 km; at 0.1 M☉, rs ≈ 0.30 km. Because distance is entered in r/rs, the normalized geometry stays stable across the whole range.
  • The Earth reference sits 26,500 ly away: rs/r < 8 × 10−17, so its gravitational dilation is negligible.

Time Dilation Factor

dτ/dt = √(1 - rs/r)
  • dτ/dt = proper time rate ratio (local time / reference time)
  • r = distance from black hole center
  • When r = rs (event horizon), dτ/dt = 0 (infinite time dilation)
  • When r → ∞ (far away), dτ/dt → 1 (normal rate)
  • The displayed TIME RATE is the probe's dτ/dt divided by the Earth observer's rate at 26,500 light-years. For any mass up to 5 M☉, the Earth factor √(1 − rs/26,500 ly) is 1.0000× to double precision. The probe therefore reads about 0.9831× at its visual baseline of 30 rs, falls to 0.0000× at the horizon (1 rs), and approaches 1× only far beyond the slider range. The 3D scene is compressed to r/rs units so WebGL stays readable; the Earth glyph at 30 scene units is a visual proxy, not a physical distance.

Kinematic (Velocity) Time Dilation — Special Relativity

Motion through space dilates time just as gravity does. In special relativity, a clock moving at velocity v relative to a stationary observer is measured to tick slower. The coordinate time Δt on the reference clock corresponding to a proper-time interval Δt0 on the moving clock is:

Δt = Δt0 / √(1 − v²/c²)

The same result expressed as the moving clock's proper-time rate (the form used by this simulator) is:

dτ/dt = √(1 − v²/c²) = 1/γ  ,  γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²)
  • v = velocity of the probe / observer relative to the reference frame (the VELOCITY slider, 0 to 1 in units of c)
  • c = speed of light = 299,792,458 m/s
  • Δt0 = proper time measured by the moving observer's own clock; Δt = time measured by the stationary reference observer
  • γ (gamma) = Lorentz factor, shown live as LORENTZ γ in the HUD
  • As v → 0, √(1 − v²/c²) → 1 and γ → 1: there is no kinematic dilation, and the simulator behaves exactly as it did before velocity was added
  • As v → c, the denominator √(1 − v²/c²) → 0, so Δt → ∞ for a fixed Δt0 and γ → ∞. From the reference frame the moving clock effectively stops; the proper-time rate falls to zero — time completely freezes
  • Scale examples: 0.5c → γ = 1.1547, probe rate 0.8660×; 0.99c → γ = 7.0888, probe rate 0.1411×; 0.999c → γ = 22.3663, probe rate 0.0447×; at exactly c the rate is 0.0000×

Combined Metric (Gravity × Velocity)

The simulator computes the probe's proper time by multiplying the gravitational Schwarzschild factor by the velocity Lorentz factor. Both effects act on the same clock, so their dilation factors combine multiplicatively:

dτ/dt = √(1 − rs/r) × √(1 − v²/c²)
  • The first factor comes from general relativity (distance r from a black hole of Schwarzschild radius rs); the second comes from special relativity (probe speed v)
  • They are independent controls: DISTANCE changes gravity, VELOCITY changes motion, and either one alone can slow the clock
  • The displayed TIME RATE is this combined probe rate divided by the reference clock's rate at 26,500 ly with the probe at rest. Since that reference rate is effectively 1, the baseline at 30 rs reads 0.9831× — physically correct for a probe that is much closer to the hole than Earth.
  • Raising VELOCITY lowers the rate even when DISTANCE never changes; the rate reaches zero if v = c or if the probe sits at the event horizon (r = rs)
  • The HUD's LORENTZ γ readout shows the velocity contribution alone, while TIME RATE shows the combined gravitational and kinematic result

Advanced Relativistic Effects

The six effects below are layered on top of the same clock model. They are visual/educational approximations, but each readout is driven by the indicated equation.

  • Gravitational & kinematic redshift — 1 + z = 1 / [√(1 − rs/r) × √(1 − v²/c²)]. The REDSHIFT (z) HUD row diverges to ∞ at the horizon or at light speed; the probe, its glow and the accretion disk shift from blue through white/yellow into deep infrared as z grows.
  • Tidal forces / spaghettification — atidal ∝ 2GM/r³. Inside 3 rs the probe is stretched radially and squeezed laterally (volume-preserving), and the TIDAL STRESS meter rises from 0% to 100% at the horizon.
  • Relativistic aberration — cos θ′ = (cos θ + β) / (1 + β cos θ). Background stars bunch toward the probe's direction of travel as velocity (or apparent orbital speed) approaches c, then snap back when it stops.
  • Frame dragging (Kerr spin) — the BLACK HOLE SPIN slider sets a = Jc/GM² from 0 to 0.99. Spacetime grid lines twist into a vortex near the center, and planets plus the probe pick up a softened Lense-Thirring tangential drift Ω ∝ a/r³.
  • Shapiro time delay — Δt ≈ (rs/c) ln(4 rE r / b²). PING LAG reports the physical round-trip delay in milliseconds; when the probe passes behind the lens (small impact parameter b) the PROBE clock visibly lags because telemetry arrives late.
  • Photon sphere ghost images — the photon ring sits at 1.5 rs. Near it, secondary/tertiary Einstein rings and mirrored Earth/galaxy ghost sprites fade in to suggest light loops around the hole.

Three-Clock System

  • LOCAL TIME — your real browser/device clock. Never affected by the simulation.
  • REFERENCE — the Earth observer's clock at 26,500 ly, ticking at √(1 − rs/26,500 ly) ≈ 1. This is the constant baseline.
  • PROBE — the observer you move, advancing at √(1 − rs/r) × √(1 − v²/c²), with r entered as r/rs. At rest at the 30 rs visual baseline it runs at 0.9831× the reference; dragged toward the horizon it slows toward 0; driven toward light speed it slows toward 0 no matter where it sits.

Visualization Notes

  • The curved spacetime surface, lensing halo and accretion disk are educational visual approximations, not a numerical GR solver.
  • Each planet carries a small spacetime dimple on the fabric whose depth follows its mass relative to Earth (Jupiter & Saturn dig deepest, Mercury barely dents it), following the classic “mass warps spacetime” picture.
  • The PULSAR is a rapidly spinning neutron star: it double-flashes and its radio-lobed beams sweep the sky, accompanied by an authentic fast damaru roll — a “dha-ta-ta-dhin” pattern at ~12 strikes per second with the deep drum (dha) and treble drum (ta) alternating (PULSAR button; real pulsars tick at milliseconds, stylized here as a drum).
  • The probe clock rate always comes from the exact dτ/dt calculation above.
  • WORMHOLE CONCEPT — THEORETICAL: the deep funnel below the event horizon is a visual metaphor for curved spacetime. It is not a claim that ordinary black holes are wormholes.

Lab Measurements

All inputs are physics-driven: DISTANCE is r/rs, MASS is 0.1–5.0 solar masses, VELOCITY is v/c, and SIMULATION SPEED only changes how fast the clocks advance on screen. No network signal or device telemetry is used.

Physical Note

This is an educational simulation. Real gravitational effects only occur in actual strong gravitational fields near massive objects. An object with mass cannot actually reach the speed of light; the VELOCITY slider explores the relativistic limiting behavior rather than a physically attainable engine.
